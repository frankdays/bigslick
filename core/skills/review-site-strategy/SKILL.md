# Review-Site Authority Strategy

Read the active client pack — category and competitors determine which grids matter. This is the third leg of the AI-citation system: review aggregators are among the most-cited sources when AI engines answer "best [category] software" queries, and review *text* (not just scores) is what gets quoted.

## 1. Platform priority

G2 first for most B2B software (largest traffic + heaviest AI citation), Capterra/GetApp for SMB-motion products, TrustRadius for enterprise depth, Gartner Peer Insights when analyst-adjacent credibility matters — at enterprise motion, coordinate Peer Insights with the analyst-relations calendar (comms lane): review volume feeds evaluation cycles. Pick 2 to run seriously; claim-and-maintain the rest.

## 2. Profile optimization

Complete every field — completeness affects ranking within category pages. Category selection is strategic: being 4th in a niche category beats 40th in a broad one, and category pages are what AI engines cite. Screenshots current, pricing transparency to the level competitors show, competitor-comparison pages claimed (G2 "X vs Y" pages rank and get cited heavily — seed your side).

## 3. Review generation engine (the compounding asset)

- **Triggers, not blasts:** ask at happiness peaks — post-onboarding success, QBR wins, high NPS, support ticket resolved with praise. Route from CRM/CS tooling (via resource-hub / MCP).
- **Volume math:** recency drives grid position; steady 5–15 reviews/month beats a 100-review burst that decays. Set a quarterly target proportional to customer base.
- **Who asks matters:** CSM ask converts best; automated email is the floor.
- **Incentives:** gift cards are platform-permitted within their rules — follow each platform's current disclosure requirements exactly; never incentivize *positive* reviews, only reviews. Never gate (screen out unhappy customers pre-ask) — it's a policy violation and it shows in patterns.
- **Coach specificity without scripting:** ask reviewers to mention their use case, role, and a measurable result — specific reviews are what AI engines quote and buyers trust. Never draft review text for a customer.

## 4. Negative reviews & competitive defense

Respond to every negative within a week: acknowledge specifically, no boilerplate, fix offline, note the fix publicly when done. A well-handled 2-star review is trust content. Monitor competitor profiles for positioning intel (their negative-review themes = your battlecard fodder → feed `competitors` skill).

## 5. Grid & badge strategy

Understand the mechanics honestly: grid position blends review quantity/recency/scores with market presence signals; paid vendor tiers buy profile features and lead access, not scores — budget accordingly and don't overpay for badges the ICP doesn't check. Badges are middle-funnel proof: deploy on pricing page, sales decks, nurture — not as a strategy in themselves.

## 6. Measurement & AI-citation loop

Quarterly: review velocity vs. target, grid movement vs. named competitors, category-page share of voice, referral traffic/leads from profiles, and — via `ai-answer-monitoring` — whether review-site content is appearing in AI answers for the client's category queries. Feed gaps back into the ask-engine (e.g., AI answers cite a competitor's integration reviews → ask integration-heavy customers next quarter).
