# Board & CEO Reporting

Read the active client pack first. Pull numbers from `pipeline-math` outputs and CRM/analytics (via resource-hub / MCP) — never invent figures; missing data becomes an explicit "we don't instrument this yet" line, which is itself credible.

## The altitude rule

Boards fund outcomes and punish surprises. Report at the level of: pipeline vs. plan, efficiency trends, material changes, and asks. Campaign details, channel anecdotes, and vanity metrics (impressions, followers, MQL counts without downstream conversion) never appear on the main slides — appendix only, and only if likely to be asked.

## Standard structure (1–3 slides + appendix)

1. **Headline** — one sentence: on/off track vs. plan and the single most important thing that changed. Never bury a miss.
2. **Pipeline scoreboard** — created vs. plan (QTD/YTD), coverage ratio for next 2 quarters, marketing-sourced % of pipeline and of closed-won. Trend arrows, not just point values.
3. **Efficiency** — CAC payback trend, pipeline $ per marketing $ spent, and one blended metric the CFO tracks (align with finance beforehand — never present a number finance will dispute live).
4. **What we're changing** — 2–3 decisions with rationale: what we're stopping, starting, or shifting budget between. This slide is what separates a leader from a reporter.
5. **Forward view** — next quarter's coverage today vs. where it needs to be, 2–3 leading indicators (pipeline creation pace, meeting volume, conversion drift) with early-warning thresholds, and anything already known to threaten the following quarter. Boards spend half the meeting here; showing up backward-only fails.
6. **Asks & risks** — what you need (budget, headcount, sales alignment, patience on a ramping channel) and the 1–2 risks to the plan you're watching.

## Pre-wiring the CFO

Before any board meeting, prep answers for the standard attacks:
- "Why is CAC up?" → decompose: mix shift vs. channel decay vs. ramp investment; show which.
- "What did we get for $X last quarter?" → pipeline created, deals influenced, payback math.
- "Why not cut brand/events/content?" → the metric each feeds and the lag before cutting shows up in pipeline.
- "Sales says the leads are bad" → conversion by source vs. agreed SLA definitions; propose the joint review rather than fighting the anecdote.
Cardinal rule: the CEO sees every number and every narrative before the board does — a surprised CEO in a board meeting is a fireable event. Reconcile every number with finance and sales ops before the meeting. Losing a number dispute in the room costs more than any bad quarter.

## Narrative discipline

- Sequence: results → cause → action. Not activity lists.
- One miss, owned plainly with a fix, buys more credibility than three excuses.
- Quarter-over-quarter consistency: same metrics, same definitions, every time. Changing metrics mid-year reads as hiding.
- Write the headline sentence FIRST; if it can't be written crisply, the analysis isn't done.

Slide-by-slide content (titles, bullets, the exact numbers with sources) ready to drop into the deck template, plus a speaker-notes paragraph per slide and the Q&A prep sheet. Offer pptx generation once content is approved.
