# PR & Analyst Relations (enterprise B2B)
Read the active client pack — AR matters in proportion to deal size and buying-committee formality; below ~$50k ACV enterprise motion, spend the effort elsewhere and say so.

## Analyst relations
**Why:** at enterprise, analysts shape shortlists before marketing ever touches the deal. AR is a sales-cycle investment, not a press program.
**The engagement ladder:**
1. **Free tier (start here):** vendor briefings — analysts accept these without a paid seat. Quarterly cadence per relevant analyst; brief on THEIR research calendar, not your launch calendar. A briefing is you talking; you get no advice back.
2. **Paid seats (ballpark tens-of-thousands per firm per year — quotes vary widely by firm and tier; verify current pricing before budgeting):** buys inquiry (you ask, they advise — the actually valuable part), strategy days, and draft-review windows. Buy when evaluation inclusion is plausible or deals cite analysts; not before.
3. **Evaluations (MQ/Wave/MarketScape):** inclusion criteria are published — revenue, customer count, capability thresholds; track your gap honestly. Cycles run on the analyst's timetable with 6–12 month runways: questionnaire → demo → customer references → draft review. The reference customers are the hidden bottleneck — build the reference bench (`case-study-builder` pipeline) two quarters before you need it.
**Discipline:** maintain the analyst map (who covers the category, their thesis, last briefing, next research), never argue draft placement emotionally — dispute facts with evidence within the review window, and remember analysts talk to your customers and competitors: message consistency is checkable.

## Press strategy (beyond launch PR)
- **News architecture:** an annual announcement calendar tiered like launches — T1 (funding, major product, flagship customers) get full press treatment; T2 get contributed articles/newsletters; T3 get owned channels only. Most "news" is T3; saying so saves money and credibility.
- **What earns coverage at this stage:** customer proof with numbers, contrarian data from your own platform, executive POV on an industry fight — not feature releases. Build the data-story muscle: one proprietary-data story per quarter outperforms four product releases.
- **Relationships over blasts:** 10 journalists/newsletters who actually cover the category, engaged between asks. Agencies amplify a strategy; they don't replace one — scope them to execution.
- **Crisis basics:** holding-statement templates pre-drafted for the 3 most plausible bad days (breach, outage, exec departure); comms owns first response (see persona-comms-manager).

## Measurement
AR: analyst mentions in deals (ask sales to log), evaluation position trajectory, inquiry usage rate on paid seats (unused seats get cut). PR: coverage in target outlets (not volume), share of voice vs. competitors in category press, citation flow into AI answers (`ai-answer-monitoring` tracks press as a source).
