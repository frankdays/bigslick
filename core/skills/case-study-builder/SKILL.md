# Case Study & Customer Story Builder

Read the active client pack — value props and ICP determine which stories are worth telling; a great story about a non-ICP customer sells to nobody you want.

## 1. Candidate selection

Score candidates on: quantifiable outcome exists (non-negotiable), ICP match, recognizable logo, champion still employed there and still happy, renewal recently closed (never mid-negotiation). Source candidates from: high NPS/CSAT responses, QBR wins, sales "who'd take a reference call" list, product usage highs.
Ask for the story at peak-happiness moments — right after a win, a renewal, or an expansion. The ask: 30-minute interview, they approve everything before anything publishes, and (sweetener) the piece doubles as internal visibility for the champion.

## 2. Interview (30 min, recorded, transcribed)

Question bank — the goal is quotes and numbers, in their words:
- Before: "What was breaking? What did it cost you — time, money, risk? What else did you try?"
- Decision: "What almost stopped you from buying? Who had to say yes? What convinced them?"
- After: "What changed, in numbers if you have them? What surprised you? What would you tell a peer considering this?"
- The kicker: "If [product] disappeared tomorrow, what happens?"
Chase numbers gently: rough ranges beat nothing ("roughly how many hours a week?"). If they can't share exact figures, negotiate an approved framing ("reduced X by roughly a third").

## 3. Story structure (write from transcript, never from imagination)

Headline = customer + quantified outcome. Then: Situation (2–3 sentences of their world) → Problem with stakes → Why they chose us (include the hesitation — it makes it credible) → Implementation (brief, honest) → Results (numbers, then a human quote) → What's next. 600–900 words for the flagship version. Every claim traceable to the transcript; every quote verbatim or approved-edited.

## 4. Approval (where case studies die — manage it like a deal)

- Send the draft with tracked scope: "reply with edits by [date]; silence on minor phrasing = OK" (agree to this upfront).
- Expect legal/comms review at enterprise customers: 2–6 weeks. Offer a pre-approved shortcut: anonymous version ("a Fortune 500 logistics company") published now, named version when cleared.
- Get written sign-off on: quotes, numbers, logo use, and WHERE it may appear (site, sales decks, ads — ads often need separate permission).
- Log approvals in the client pack's proof library with date and scope — and track usage: the same champions become sales references, and burning the same 6 logos across every deal and asset exhausts goodwill. Rotate; flag any reference used >1x/month to the CSM.

## 5. Repurpose (one interview → 10 assets)

Flagship written study → 1-page sales PDF → 3 pull-quote graphics → 2 LinkedIn posts (champion tagged, if approved) → nurture email → website proof-bar stat → sales-deck slide → objection-handling snippet mapped to the objection it answers → (if the champion agrees) webinar or podcast guest slot.
Map each asset to funnel stage and persona; a proof point only works when it matches the reader's situation.
