# Field Marketing & Events

Read the active client pack first — ACV and sales motion determine whether events make sense at all (rule of thumb: field events need ACV roughly >$25k to pay back; below that, webinars and digital carry the load).

## Portfolio design (annual/semi-annual)

Tier the portfolio before picking any single event:
- **T1 — Major trade shows** (2–4/yr max): where the ICP already gathers. Decision inputs: attendee-list overlap with ICP, competitor presence, historical sourced pipeline. Booth is optional; meetings are not.
- **T2 — Sponsored conferences/regional**: smaller checks, speaking slot or dinner attached beats logo-only every time. Kill pure-logo sponsorships.
- **T3 — Owned field events**: exec dinners (8–12 attendees, 50/50 customers:prospects), roadshows, customer days. Highest cost-per-attendee, highest conversion — reserve for named-account/ABM lists.
- **Webinars**: the always-on layer. Monthly cadence beats sporadic; a series with a POV beats one-off product demos. Repurpose every recording (clips, posts, nurture content).

Budget allocation guardrail: if events are >25% of program budget, the measurement section below is mandatory before adding anything.
Cost reality: budget T1/T2 events at fully-loaded cost — sponsorship fee typically ~50% of true cost once booth services, travel, shipping, and staff time land. Plans built on sticker price blow the lane budget by summer. Exec dinners require a genuine exec host committed at booking time; no exec, no dinner.
Lead-time reality: T1 booths and speaking slots book 6–12 months out — the annual portfolio decision happens in Q3–Q4 for the following year.

## Per-event playbook (the 3-phase rule: an event is 20% show, 40% before, 40% after)

**Before (6–8 wks out):** pipeline goal in $ and meetings (not leads), target-account meeting list built with sales, 3-touch pre-book sequence (email + LinkedIn + sales call), staffing/talk-track brief, offer for booking (exec time, roadmap preview, dinner seat — not swag).
**During:** meeting schedule owns the calendar; badge scans are tier-3 output. Daily debrief: what's landing, who to chase tomorrow.
**After (the phase that determines ROI):** 48-hour SLA on meeting follow-ups, 1-week SLA on all other contacts, sequenced by conversation quality not scan order. Route hot contacts to sales with context notes. 30-day pipeline check written into the calendar at booking time.

**Webinar sub-playbook:** 3-week promotion window; registration→attendance expect 35–50%; the replay audience usually exceeds live — gate the replay decision on funnel goals; follow-up segments: attended/registered-no-show/asked-a-question (the last is a sales-ready signal).

## Measurement

Per event, track: cost, meetings held, opportunities created (90-day window), pipeline $ sourced and influenced, cost per opportunity. Compare cost-per-opp against paid channels each quarter — events survive on this comparison or get cut. Attribute honestly: influenced ≠ sourced; report both, never blend them.
Annual review: rank every event by cost-per-opp, kill the bottom quartile, reinvest in the top.
